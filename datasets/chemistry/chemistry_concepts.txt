Chemistry is the study of matter, its properties, composition and transformations.

Atoms are the basic units of matter and consist of protons, neutrons and electrons.

Elements are substances consisting of atoms with the same atomic number.

Compounds are substances formed when two or more elements chemically combine in fixed proportions.

Molecules are groups of atoms bonded together.

The atomic number of an element is the number of protons in its nucleus.

The mass number is the total number of protons and neutrons in an atom.

Isotopes are atoms of the same element having different numbers of neutrons.

Ions are charged atoms or groups of atoms formed by gaining or losing electrons.

A mole is a unit representing a fixed number of particles, approximately 6.022e23.

Molar mass is the mass of one mole of a substance.

Stoichiometry is the quantitative study of reactants and products in chemical reactions.

A chemical equation represents a chemical reaction using chemical formulas and coefficients.

Acids are substances that increase hydrogen ion concentration in aqueous solution.

Bases are substances that increase hydroxide ion concentration or accept protons.

pH measures the acidity or basicity of an aqueous solution.

A neutral solution has equal concentrations of hydrogen and hydroxide ions.

Oxidation is the loss of electrons.

Reduction is the gain of electrons.

A redox reaction contains both oxidation and reduction.

Chemical equilibrium occurs when forward and reverse reaction rates become equal.

A catalyst changes reaction rate by providing an alternative pathway with lower activation energy.

Activation energy is the minimum energy required for a reaction to occur.

Molarity is the number of moles of solute per liter of solution.

Density is mass per unit volume.

The ideal gas law is PV = nRT.

Kinetic energy in chemistry refers to the energy associated with particle motion.

Exothermic reactions release heat to the surroundings.

Endothermic reactions absorb heat from the surroundings.

A limiting reagent is the reactant that is consumed first in a chemical reaction.

Theoretical yield is the maximum amount of product predicted by stoichiometry.

Actual yield is the amount of product actually obtained experimentally.

Percentage yield compares actual yield with theoretical yield.

Electrochemistry studies chemical reactions involving electron transfer and electrical energy.

Organic chemistry primarily studies carbon-containing compounds.

Inorganic chemistry studies compounds and elements outside the main scope of organic chemistry.

Physical chemistry applies physical principles to understand chemical systems.

Analytical chemistry deals with identifying and measuring chemical substances.

The periodic table organizes elements according to atomic number and recurring chemical properties.

Groups are vertical columns in the periodic table.

Periods are horizontal rows in the periodic table.

Metals generally conduct heat and electricity and tend to lose electrons.

Nonmetals generally have poor electrical conductivity and tend to gain or share electrons.

Noble gases are highly unreactive elements in group 18.

Alkali metals are highly reactive elements in group 1.

Alkaline earth metals belong to group 2.

Halogens belong to group 17 and are highly reactive nonmetals.

Chemical bonds include ionic, covalent and metallic bonding.

Ionic bonding involves electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions.

Covalent bonding involves sharing of electrons between atoms.

A polar covalent bond has unequal sharing of electrons.

A nonpolar covalent bond has approximately equal sharing of electrons.

Intermolecular forces act between molecules.

Hydrogen bonding is a relatively strong intermolecular interaction involving hydrogen bonded to highly electronegative atoms.

Solubility is the ability of a substance to dissolve in a solvent.

A solution contains a solute dissolved in a solvent.

Concentration describes the amount of solute present in a given amount of solution.

Le Chatelier's principle describes how an equilibrium system responds to changes in conditions.

Reaction rate describes how quickly reactants are converted into products.

Increasing temperature generally increases the rate of many chemical reactions.

Increasing reactant concentration can increase reaction rate.

A balanced chemical equation obeys conservation of atoms.

Coefficients in balanced equations represent mole ratios.
