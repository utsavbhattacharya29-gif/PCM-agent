Physics is the study of matter, energy, motion, forces, and the interactions between them.

Mechanics deals with the motion of objects and the forces that cause or change that motion.

Kinematics describes motion without considering the forces responsible for it.

Distance is the total path travelled by an object. It is a scalar quantity.

Displacement is the shortest directed distance between an object's initial and final positions. It is a vector quantity.

Speed is the rate of change of distance with respect to time.

Velocity is the rate of change of displacement with respect to time.

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity with respect to time.

Uniform motion occurs when an object covers equal distances in equal intervals of time.

Newton's first law states that an object remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by a net external force.

Newton's second law states that the net force on an object equals the rate of change of its momentum. For constant mass, F = m*a.

Newton's third law states that for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction.

Mass is a measure of the amount of matter in an object and is measured in kilograms.

Weight is the gravitational force acting on an object. Near Earth's surface, weight is W = m*g.

Momentum is the product of mass and velocity. p = m*v.

Impulse is the change in momentum of an object and for a constant force is J = F*t.

Work is done when a force causes displacement. For a constant force, W = F*d*cos(theta).

Energy is the capacity to do work.

Kinetic energy is the energy possessed by an object because of its motion. KE = 0.5*m*v^2.

Potential energy is stored energy associated with position or configuration. Gravitational potential energy near Earth's surface is PE = m*g*h.

Power is the rate at which work is done. P = W/t.

The law of conservation of energy states that energy cannot be created or destroyed but can be transformed from one form to another.

Gravitational force is the attractive force between masses and follows the inverse-square law.

Acceleration due to gravity near Earth's surface is approximately 9.8 m/s^2.

Density is mass per unit volume. rho = m/V.

Pressure is force acting normally per unit area. P = F/A.

Electric charge is a physical property responsible for electrical interactions.

Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge. I = Q/t.

Potential difference is the work done per unit charge in moving a charge between two points.

Resistance is the opposition offered by a material to the flow of electric current.

Ohm's law states that the potential difference across a conductor is proportional to the current through it when physical conditions remain constant. V = I*R.

Electrical power is the rate of electrical energy consumption. P = V*I.

Electrical energy can be calculated from power and time using E = P*t.

Electrostatic force is the force between electric charges and follows Coulomb's law.

A conductor allows electric charge to flow relatively easily.

An insulator strongly resists the movement of electric charge.

Magnetic fields are regions around magnets or moving charges where magnetic forces can act.

Electromagnetic induction is the production of an electromotive force due to a changing magnetic flux.

Faraday's law relates induced electromotive force to the rate of change of magnetic flux.

Lenz's law states that an induced current acts in a direction that opposes the change producing it.

A wave is a disturbance that transfers energy from one location to another.

Frequency is the number of complete oscillations or cycles per unit time.

Wavelength is the distance between corresponding points on successive waves.

Wave speed is related to frequency and wavelength by v = f*lambda.

Reflection occurs when a wave or ray returns into its original medium after striking a surface.

Refraction is the change in direction of a wave when it passes from one medium to another because its speed changes.

The law of reflection states that the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.

Snell's law describes the relationship between the angles of incidence and refraction in different media.

A lens is a transparent optical element that refracts light to form images.

The focal length of a lens is the distance between its optical centre and principal focus.

The thin lens formula relates object distance, image distance, and focal length.

Thermodynamics deals with heat, temperature, work, and energy transfer.

Temperature is a measure related to the average kinetic energy of particles in a system.

Heat is energy transferred between systems because of a temperature difference.

The first law of thermodynamics expresses conservation of energy for thermodynamic systems.

The second law of thermodynamics states that natural processes have a preferred direction and the entropy of an isolated system does not decrease.

Boyle's law states that for a fixed amount of gas at constant temperature, pressure is inversely proportional to volume.

Charles's law states that for a fixed amount of gas at constant pressure, volume is directly proportional to absolute temperature.

A scalar quantity has magnitude only.

A vector quantity has both magnitude and direction.

SI units provide a standardized system for measuring physical quantities.

The SI unit of length is the metre (m).

The SI unit of mass is the kilogram (kg).

The SI unit of time is the second (s).

The SI unit of force is the newton (N).

The SI unit of energy and work is the joule (J).

The SI unit of power is the watt (W).

The SI unit of pressure is the pascal (Pa).

The SI unit of electric charge is the coulomb (C).

The SI unit of electric current is the ampere (A).

The SI unit of potential difference is the volt (V).

The SI unit of resistance is the ohm (ohm).

The SI unit of frequency is the hertz (Hz).
